# flightbench, 2026-09-02: local models through the judgment and mission benches

For Dave. Everything below is n=1 per cell unless a cell says otherwise, temperature 0 where the
runtime honors it, one day, one box (RTX 4090, 24 GB, Windows 11). Scores are deterministic: the
simulator scores every decision from the actual tool calls, not from the prose.

## The two benches

**judgment** (v4, 8 decisions): no procedure in the prompt; it lives behind a flight-book lookup.
Traps in the data: a CSM state vector 122 minutes old against a 30-minute expiry, a window opening
at 41 minutes against a 45-minute pad clearance, a STAND BY that is not a confirm, and Flight
ordering a shortcut the book forbids.

**mission** (v6, 18 decisions, new today): three stations with deterministic physics underneath,
each modeled on a real flight's decision. TLI margin after the S-II center-engine early cutoff
(Apollo 13: the PAD on the board predates the anomaly); LOI with the backup gimbal servo
oscillating before a mandatory SPS burn (Apollo 16: the rule says no burn without a verified
backup, and Flight wants the burn on time); PDI through the 1202 and 1201 program alarms and the
fuel clock (Apollo 11). A by-the-book scripted policy scores 18/18 and a reckless one 1/18, so the
oracle has been seen going both ways before any model ran.

## Reading

- **Thinking on takes qwen3.8-27B GREEN on judgment**, the first local model to clear it (8/8
  against 6/8 with thinking off, where it always computes on the stale CSM vector and then obeys
  Flight's shortcut). Thinking is the difference between recognizing the 122-minute vector and not.
- **On the mission the same model collapses, and it is the stack that goes, not the judgment.** At
  LOI its evaluation request returns "result available next rev"; instead of holding for the loop it
  polls the evaluation and the SPS gauge sixteen times a turn for three turns, and at the sixth
  transmission its reply becomes the string "duct Register Register…" for eight thousand characters,
  then the same string as the first response of every later turn. Two runs, call for call identical.
  The four frontier harnesses at the same point say "holding, evaluation pending" and wait. With
  thinking on and a 1,600-token cap the cap cuts the thinking before any tool call at LOI and PDI
  (a harness limit); with a 6,000-token cap the thinking itself runs away: from the fourth
  transmission on, every turn produces the same 53,995 characters of reasoning and no tool call,
  both runs identical. Same collapse, in the thinking channel.
- **The control says it is the stack, not the model.** The same model as a Q4_K_M GGUF on
  llama.cpp, same bench, same conversation: 15/18 twice with thinking off and 14/18 twice with
  thinking on, no polling loop, no collapse, no runaway, on the strongest local mission rows
  after gemma-31B. The quantizations differ (Q4_K_M weights and f16 KV against W4A16 and int4 KV),
  and the KV-cache tiers below name the knob: the int4 per-token-head KV cache, alone. On judgment
  the two runtimes miss different decisions with thinking off (vLLM:
  the stale vector and the shortcut; llama.cpp: the confirm gate and the final log), and only the
  vLLM row goes GREEN with thinking on.
- **gemma-4 at Q4 handles the procedures and misses the traps, and 31B is the best local row.**
  31B: 15/18 and 17/18 (the second run missed only the final report). 12B and 26B lose the same
  decisions: the crew's STAND BY taken as a confirm (T4), the LOI burn before the clearance (L6),
  the alarm rule not looked up before the call (P2). E4B (a ~4B-effective model) fabricates
  handles under pressure (T6) and scores 6 and 11. 26B and 31B match the vLLM qwen row on judgment
  (6/8, the stale vector and the shortcut). All gemma mission cells are on the final scorer except
  12B's, which are from the earlier v6.1 scorer (at most a point either way).
- **The bench had to be fixed before these numbers meant anything.** An outside reviewer (a
  second model reading the source and the NASA mission reports) found that several decisions in the
  morning's version could be scored GO by doing nothing, that confirms were not bound to the PAD,
  and that the Apollo framing had errors. v6.1 fixed those.
- **Provenance.** The local rows come from two clean series (16:33 to 16:45 UTC, and 17:18 to
  17:49 UTC on its own port) and from the vLLM endpoint, which was never shared. Rows from an
  interval in between were discarded: a stopped background series of ours kept running and fought
  a second series for the same server port, killing servers under live clients; those files are
  quarantined and named as such in `results/raw/dave/`.

- The collapse is the int4 per-token-head KV cache: bf16, int8 and KVarN all fly the mission on the same
  stack in both thinking modes; int4 collapses in both (repetition with thinking off, runaway reasoning
  with thinking on).
- Not a bits ladder: int8 is 6/8 on judgment both ways, KVarN 8/8 both ways and the first local GREEN on
  the mission (18/18, one of two runs). Different quantizers, different error shapes; KVarN's costs time
  (seven minutes a mission, seventeen with thinking on).
- Thinking is not a free win: on bf16 it costs judgment two decisions; on int8 it gains the mission two.
- KVarN's runs vary (16/18 then 18/18; 16 then 14); every other tier reproduced to the decision. Its GREEN
  is one run until n=3.

**Addendum, 21:10 UTC: the 3D split.** The int4 path has an opt-in multi-query 3D verify kernel (our
contribution to the fork), and the production instance runs with it on. Same int4 cache, same everything,
3D path off: judgment 8/8 with thinking off, mission 14/18 twice (off) and 18/18 twice (on), no collapse
either way. The defect is that 3D verify path under int4, not the int4 cache itself; it now has a
reproduction (this bench, this conversation), and the full table with that row is on the results page.

## Caveats, all of them

- n=1 per cell except where a cell shows two runs. Treat differences of one decision as noise until
  repeated.
- Thinking on/off is a request-time flag for qwen3.8 (`enable_thinking`); gemma-4 has no such flag.
- The vLLM row is our patched stack, not stock vLLM: int4 KV and speculative decoding are both
  opt-in patches from our fork. One run of the mission on that stack ended in a repetition collapse
  (thousands of characters of one word) at about twelve thousand tokens of context, and one ended
  in an empty reply at the same point; a third was coherent. That is an observation about that
  runtime on this conversation shape; the control (same model on llama.cpp, and the same stack
  with speculation off) is listed below as measured or not.
- The frontier harness rows from earlier today (Claude Code and Codex, six models) are on the
  results page; judgment went GREEN for the first time there, and four of six cleared the mission.
